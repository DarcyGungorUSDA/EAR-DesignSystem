# How much do you want to know?

## The basics
We're using Arial because it's a sans-serif typeface available in both MS Office and Adobe without any of us needing to download anything. 

## Beyond the basics

### Why not source sans pro?

In an ideal world, we'd use source sans for a consistent look and feel with the FNA website, which uses source sans pro. Unfortunately, while the whole source sans pro font family is available to FNA within MS Office, it's not available to us in Adobe Acrobat [even though it's an Adobe font](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/source-sans) - Adobe Acrobat gives us a few source sans 3 fonts, but not the regular font. 

<img scr="images/SourceSansAcrobat.png" alt="The source sans fonts available to FNA through Adobe Acrobat are source sans 3 black, extra bold, extra light, medium, and semi-bold. width=400>

This means if we convert a Word document to pdf and realize we need to edit a few words, we're in a tight spot. It would be great to open the Acrobat text editor and just do it there - but the edited words will appear in a different font. And, it's a hassle to go back to the Word doc and re-convert to pdf for small edits.

CEC isn't able to provide us with an FNA-wide solution (... we tried when developing the FNA PowerPoint template) and it's a lot to assume that every EAR employee will 

So, the best option is to pick a font that's available to FNA in MS Office and Adobe Acrobat without anyone needing to download anything.
